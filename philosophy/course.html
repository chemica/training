Foundation course · Read, listen, practise

Philosophy for a deliberate worldview

Ten short lessons for clearer reasoning, defensible beliefs, moral judgment, and charitable debate.

Work in order. Each lesson ends with something to send back to your teacher for feedback. Listening builds familiarity; answering from memory builds durable knowledge.

  1. 1. Break an argument without breaking the conversation

    Premises, conclusions, reconstruction, and hidden bridges.

  2. 2. Truth here. Truth everywhere.

    Validity, soundness, necessity, and the countermodel test.

  3. 3. Three ways reasons can carry you

    Deduction, induction, and inference to the best explanation.

  4. 4. Compile a claim before you test it

    Propositions, connectives, scope, and necessary or sufficient conditions.

  5. 5. Make every case show its hand

    Truth tables, tautologies, contradictions, and exhaustive validity tests.

  6. 6. When a true belief is not enough

    Knowledge, justification, epistemic luck, and defeaters.

  7. 7. See a moral decision through three lenses

    Consequences, duties, character, and responsible moral deliberation.

  8. 8. What are we doing when we call something wrong?

    Realism, error theory, expressivism, relativism, and constructivism.

  9. 9. Map reality beyond the actual

    Ontology, identity, grounding, necessity, and possible worlds.

  10. 10. Audit a worldview without rigging the test

    The strongest pressures for and against theism, atheism, and naturalism.
