Shared reference

Trading terms, used precisely.

A compact vocabulary for the course. Lessons define only the terms needed for their single decision.

Acceptance
Sustained trade beyond a relevant area, supported by closes, time and structural progress.
Adverse selection
Being filled when the counterparty is more motivated or informed, so price soon moves against the fill.
Basis point
0.01 percentage points; 100 basis points equal 1%.
Context
Market regime and structural location on the timeframe assigned to that job.
Depth
Displayed quantity available at successive bid and ask prices on one venue.
Expectancy
Average net outcome per attempt: win probability × average win minus loss probability × average loss.
Friction
Fees, spread, slippage and impact separating gross movement from realised outcome.
Invalidation
Observable market evidence contradicting the trade thesis.
Liquidity
Ability to execute a given size promptly with limited price concession.
Market regime
A useful classification of current behaviour, such as directional, rotational or transitional, combined with volatility.
Pivot
A local turning point confirmed by price moving away.
Rejection
A probe into or through an area followed by movement away; evidence, not a guaranteed reversal.
R-multiple
Outcome divided by initial planned risk; +2R gains twice the amount initially risked.
Setup
A conditional opportunity created by context and location; not permission to enter.
Slippage
Difference between expected execution and the realised average fill.
Spread
Difference between the best displayed bid and ask on a venue.
Trigger
Observable evidence required before taking a planned action.
Volume
Quantity already traded over an interval; not the same as current liquidity.
