Lesson 0008 · 30 minutes · One decision

Classify breach, acceptance or failure.

By the end: After a boundary breach, classify acceptance, rejection or unresolved using subsequent behaviour.

Boundary: all charts and tickets are historical, replayed or paper-only. Judge the decision made with information available then—not the eventual P&L.

0–5 min · The invariant

Crossing a boundary is an event. Acceptance or failure is a sequence: closes, time beyond, retest behaviour and structural progress supply evidence.

Breach
Price trades beyond a boundary.
Acceptance
Trade sustains beyond and builds structure away.
Failed breakout
Breach returns inside and cannot re-establish outside.
Retest
Revisit of the boundary from the other side.

Open the shared glossary if a supporting term is unfamiliar.

5–18 min · One structure, five outcomes

Read each chart left to right. The paths are schematics, not predictions; compare the decisive evidence rather than memorising their silhouettes.

CanonicalExample 1

Price closes above, holds a retest and makes a new swing high.

Decision: Acceptance.

Visual variationExample 2

No neat retest; three overlapping closes remain above before expansion.

Decision: Messy appearance, still acceptance outside.

Opposite resolutionExample 3

Price wicks above, closes inside, retests from below and rotates lower.

Decision: Failed breakout/rejection.

Near missExample 4

A single close above is immediately labelled acceptance.

Decision: Premature; a breach plus one close may remain unresolved.

AmbiguousExample 5

Multiple candles straddle the boundary with no progress either way.

Decision: Unresolved; neither branch activates.

18–24 min · Unseen decision

After a breach, price spends four candles above resistance but each closes lower; the retest has not occurred. Best label?

24–30 min · Deliberate practice

At five historical breaches, write two branches before reveal: acceptance trigger/invalidation and failure trigger/invalidation. Reveal one bar at a time; record which branch activates, or neither.

Save one pre-outcome screenshot and your classification. Ask your LLM teacher: “Which observation decided the classification? What would falsify it? Ignore the eventual profit.”
