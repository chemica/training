Lesson 0009 · 30 minutes · Playbook · Paper only

The middle pays no rent.

Today’s win: frame a range as a conditional auction, plan a boundary-to-interior rotation, and recognise when acceptance invalidates the fade.

Training constraint: make every decision in replay or on paper. Crypto trades continuously, across fragmented venues, and can move sharply. This teaches observation and risk reasoning—not a promise of profit or a recommendation to trade.

0–4 · Retrieve first

Why does entering near the middle of a range usually weaken both invalidation clarity and reward-to-risk?

4–10 · A precise vocabulary

A range is not any sideways patch. It is a working hypothesis: price has repeatedly travelled between two zones, and neither side has sustained control beyond them.

Boundary

A zone—not an exact pixel—where prior continuation attempts met opposition. Evidence matters most at the upper and lower boundaries.

Equilibrium / midpoint

The 50% level between the chosen boundaries. It is a geometric reference, not proof of fair value or equal traded volume.

Rejection

A failed attempt to remain beyond a boundary followed by an observable return inside. A wick is evidence; follow-through makes it stronger.

Rotation

Directional travel from one area of the range toward another—edge to midpoint, or edge to edge. It is a path, not a guarantee.

Acceptance

Sustained trade outside: closes and time beyond the boundary, retests that hold, or structure forming away from the old range.

Mean reversion

The hypothesis that displacement from a reference tends to return toward it. Here the reference is the range interior; the regime must persist.

10–14 · Read the auction

Work large to small: decide whether the higher-timeframe market is balanced or trending; mark the range on the decision timeframe; use a lower timeframe only for the trigger. Do not let lower-timeframe noise invent the range.

14–19 · Boundary-fade playbook

  1. Context: require two meaningful responses from each zone and travel through the interior. Exclude obvious one-way expansion.
  2. Location: wait at an edge. Passing through the midpoint is normal; initiating there rarely offers asymmetric location.
  3. Trigger: require observable rejection—for example, excursion, close back inside, then failure to regain the excursion high.
  4. Invalidation: define acceptance beforehand, such as two closes outside plus a retest that holds. Never widen it after entry.
  5. Target: treat the midpoint as the first decision point. Plan the far edge only if rotation remains clean.
  6. Time stop: if price cannot leave the boundary within a set number of bars, the expected rotation is absent.
net paper expectancy = (win rate × average win) − (loss rate × average loss) − friction

Friction includes spread, fees, and slippage. Record outcomes in R, where 1R is the planned loss from entry to invalidation. Reject setups where plausible round-trip friction consumes too much of the target.

19–22 · Worked paper example

Hypothetical BTC range: £49,200–£50,000; midpoint £49,600. Price prints £50,080, closes back at £49,940, then fails to exceed £50,020.

Plan

Paper entry £49,920; structural stop £50,100; first target £49,600. Risk is £180/BTC and gross reward £320/BTC: 1.78R before friction.

Size

With a hypothetical £10 risk budget: £10 ÷ £180 = 0.0556 BTC. Size follows invalidation distance; the stop is not moved to manufacture size.

Fork

£49,600 completes the first rotation. Two closes above £50,000 and a held retest indicate acceptance: cancel or exit by the prewritten rule.

One attractive example proves nothing. A distribution of paper outcomes, including costs and missed fills, is needed to estimate whether the playbook has any edge.

22–25 · Failure modes

  • Fading pressure: repeated shallow pullbacks into one edge may signal expansion, not resilience.
  • Redrawing afterward: moving a boundary so every overshoot becomes “rejection” destroys falsifiability.
  • Averaging into acceptance: adding because price is “more stretched” increases exposure as the regime may be changing.
  • Automatic far-edge target: the midpoint or internal structure may halt the rotation.
  • Ignoring conditions: news, liquidations, outages, thin books, and fragmented pricing can abruptly end balance.

25–28 · Timed replay

Use a liquid crypto pair in historical replay, paper-only, and one venue for candles and friction estimates.

  1. 2 min: find a completed period with two responses at each boundary; hide later bars.
  2. 2 min: record zones, midpoint, width, and why this is a range rather than a trend pause.
  3. 3 min: at the next edge test, write trigger, entry, acceptance-based invalidation, first target, time stop, and friction.
  4. 3 min: reveal bars singly. Label each rejection, acceptance, or undecided; record maximum favourable/adverse excursion in R.

LLM feedback artefact: send the hidden-outcome screenshot and plan. Ask: “Was the range prospective? Is rejection observable? Would invalidation detect acceptance? Are costs and time represented? Which rule permits hindsight?”

28–30 · Retrieval test

1. Price wicks above the upper edge and closes barely inside. What is established?

2. Why is the midpoint a decision point, not a guaranteed target?

3. Two closes occur above the range and a pullback holds above the old edge. Which hypothesis gains support?

Self-check rubric

You are ready to practise when you can define the range before the test, distinguish rejection from acceptance observably, calculate gross R and friction, and cancel a fade without arguing with expansion. Otherwise repeat one replay—not a live trade.
