Lesson 0003 · 30 minutes · Paper trading only

Describe structure without predicting it.

Today’s win: turn a noisy BTC chart into a defensible sequence of swings, then distinguish trend, range, and transition without reacting to every candle.

This chapter trains observation, not forecasting. Use chart replay or paper trading. Crypto markets are volatile, and leverage can amplify losses; the CFTC warns that speculation in virtual-currency futures and options is high risk. CFTC customer advisory.

0–4 · Retrieve before reading

Without looking below, write one sentence for each prompt.

  1. What makes a high or low structurally meaningful?
  2. Define an uptrend without saying “bullish,” “momentum,” or “moving average.”
  3. What is the difference between a range and a transition?

4–10 · A precise vocabulary

Structure is a compressed description of where price repeatedly changed direction. These are operational definitions for consistent annotation; traders may use different algorithms, so record yours before comparing charts.

Pivot high

A bar or small cluster whose high is exceeded by neither a chosen number of bars immediately before nor immediately after it. A pivot low reverses the comparison. It is local and only confirms after the right-hand bars exist.

Swing

A selected pivot that anchors a visible directional leg. Every swing contains a pivot, but not every tiny pivot deserves swing status. Selection must use one consistent sensitivity.

Leg

The movement from one selected swing to the next. An impulse leg travels with the current directional sequence; a pullback leg travels against it.

StateOperational definitionWhat would challenge it?
UptrendAt least two selected swing highs and two selected swing lows advance: HH and HL sequences.A break below the last protected swing low damages it; follow-through is still required for a downtrend.
DowntrendAt least two selected swing highs and two selected swing lows decline: LH and LL sequences.A break above the last protected swing high damages it; one break alone does not prove reversal.
RangeRepeated rotations overlap between an upper and lower zone, without sustained closes and follow-through outside.Acceptance beyond a boundary, followed by a pullback that holds outside it.
TransitionThe previous sequence has been invalidated or weakened, but a new trend or stable range is not yet confirmed.Formation of a fresh ordered sequence or repeated range rotations.

The protected low in an uptrend is the swing low whose subsequent advance produced a new swing high; reverse the idea for a protected high in a downtrend. It is a useful reference, not a guaranteed stop level. CME’s education material likewise presents prior highs/lows as support and resistance and stresses that these are usually zones rather than exact prices. CME: Support and Resistance.

10–15 · Read the sequence, one leg at a time

Answer: the visible sequence is an uptrend; HL is the protected low because the advance from it produced the newest HH. The revealed lower low breaks that protection. Label the immediate state transition, not downtrend: no lower-high/lower-low sequence exists yet.

15–19 · Worked example

Suppose a 1-hour BTC chart yields selected swings at £48,000 low → £50,400 high → £49,100 low → £52,000 high → £50,600 low → £53,200 high.

  1. Compare highs: £50,400 → £52,000 → £53,200. They advance.
  2. Compare lows: £48,000 → £49,100 → £50,600. They advance.
  3. Classify: uptrend. The £50,600 low is protected after price establishes £53,200 as a swing high.
  4. Update, do not predict: price then trades to £50,300. The old uptrend is damaged. Until a rebound forms a lower high and price subsequently makes a lower low—or a different stable pattern forms—the disciplined label is transition.

Common error: re-labelling every shallow turn. Increase your swing threshold until the marks remain broadly useful after one small zoom change. “Maximum six visible swings” is a training constraint, not a universal market law.

19–27 · Eight-minute replay drill

Use a 1-hour BTC spot chart in replay mode. Hide at least 30 future bars. No orders, leverage, or real money.

  1. Minute 0–1: Write your selection rule—for example, “only turns followed by a move at least as large as the recent typical hourly range.” Avoid changing it mid-round.
  2. Minute 1–3: Mark no more than six confirmed swings. Label HH, HL, LH, or LL by comparison with the previous swing of the same type.
  3. Minute 3–4: State one classification: uptrend, downtrend, range, or transition. Quote the swing sequence as evidence.
  4. Minute 4–7: Reveal one bar at a time. Never move an old mark. Add a provisional mark only after price moves away; record each state change and the exact evidence.
  5. Minute 7–8: Score process, not P&L: one point each for consistent threshold, ≤6 marks, evidence-based label, unchanged historical marks, and a correctly provisional newest pivot.

27–30 · Test yourself

  1. A three-bar pivot-high rule is used. Can the current bar be a confirmed pivot high? Why?
  2. Highs are 100, 105, 109; lows are 94, 97, 101. Classify the sequence.
  3. An uptrend’s protected low breaks. Must the chart now be a downtrend?
  4. Price crosses a range high for one candle, then closes back inside. What evidence is missing before calling a breakout?
  5. Your labels change whenever you zoom one step. What should you change first?
Answer key and feedback
  1. No. A pivot needing three bars on its right cannot confirm until those bars exist. Calling it confirmed uses future information.
  2. Uptrend. Both comparable highs and lows advance. State the sequence, rather than relying on the chart’s visual slope.
  3. No. The break invalidates or damages the prior uptrend; it creates transition first. A declining high-and-low sequence would support downtrend.
  4. Acceptance and follow-through. Look for sustained closes outside, or an outside retest that holds. A brief excursion may be rejection.
  5. Reduce sensitivity. Select fewer, larger pivots with one explicit threshold. Do not retroactively move earlier marks to improve the story.
