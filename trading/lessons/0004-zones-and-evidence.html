Lesson 0004 · 30 minutes · Paper practice only

Make levels earn their place.

Today’s win: turn a vague “support/resistance” impression into a bounded zone, then predefine the observations that count as rejection, acceptance, or no decision.

Safety boundary. This is chart-reading practice, not a signal. Use replay or paper trading only. Crypto trades continuously, can move sharply, and may trade differently across venues. The CFTC warns that virtual-currency speculation is high risk and leverage amplifies losses as well as gains. CFTC advisory.

0–4 · Retrieve before reading

Without checking earlier lessons, answer briefly.

4–10 · The vocabulary of a zone

Support

An area below current price where buying or reduced selling has previously interrupted a decline. It describes location and prior behaviour—not a promise.

Resistance

An area above current price where selling or reduced buying has previously interrupted an advance. The same location can later behave differently.

Zone

A bounded price interval chosen from visible structure. Its width acknowledges dispersion among fills, wicks, closes, venues, and timeframes.

Rejection

An attempted move into or through the zone followed by movement away. A wick is evidence; a close back plus follow-through is stronger evidence.

Acceptance

Sustained trade on the far side: typically closes, time, and continued two-way trade beyond the boundary. It is a working inference, not a fact one indicator proves.

Role reversal

After price crosses a zone, former resistance may be tested as support, or vice versa. The retest supplies new evidence; the label alone supplies none.

CME defines support and resistance as places where price may slow or reverse, notes that prior highs and lows are common sources, and explicitly cautions that these areas do not hold “to the penny.” That is why this lesson uses intervals. CME: Support and Resistance.

Observation is not explanation

“Three 1-hour candles closed back below 64,300” is an observation. “Smart money defended resistance” is a story. Stories may aid memory, but they are not directly testable. Prefer language another observer could score from the same candles. A zone is a place to ask a question: what does price do here?

10–14 · Read the evidence sequence

  1. Candidate: visible prior swing, repeated pause, range edge, or high-volume consolidation—not merely a round number noticed afterward.
  2. Approach: record speed, candle overlap, and whether repeated tests produce weaker or stronger departures. Approach changes the question, not the answer.
  3. Reaction: note wick penetration, closing location, displacement away, and the next candle; do not count touches mechanically.
  4. Acceptance: price closes beyond the zone and continues trading there. More time and follow-through strengthen the inference, but no universal candle count exists.
  5. Role test: price revisits from the other side. A hold supports role reversal; decisive movement back through weakens it.
Ambiguity matters. Repeated tests can reveal interest, consume resting liquidity, or simply reflect balance. Candles do not reveal intention. Judge the response and keep an explicit “unclear” state.

14–18 · Construct a defensible zone

  1. Choose the source. Start with a conspicuous swing or compact consolidation on the decision timeframe.
  2. Set bounds. Use the cluster of bodies/closes for one boundary and relevant rejection extremes for the other. If one abnormal wick makes the zone unusably wide, record it separately rather than silently ignoring it.
  3. Measure width. Write both prices and width as a percentage so visual zoom cannot change your judgement.
  4. Write evidence rules. Define rejection, acceptance, and no-decision before advancing replay.
  5. Write deletion criteria. Examples: decisive acceptance through the area, distance from current price, or later structure making it irrelevant.

zone width % = (upper bound − lower bound) ÷ midpoint × 100

A higher-timeframe zone should usually survive a move to a lower timeframe. The lower timeframe may reveal execution detail, but repeatedly redrawing higher-timeframe boundaries around later candles is hindsight fitting.

18–22 · Worked BTC/USD replay example

Fictional data: on 4-hour, BTC/USD previously rallied into 64,100–64,500, printed several upper wicks, and sold to 61,800. Current price approaches from below. Midpoint = 64,300; width = 400 ÷ 64,300 ≈ 0.62%.

Prewritten evidence:

  • Rejection candidate: trade above 64,100, then a 4-hour close back below 64,100.
  • Stronger rejection: the next candle fails to reclaim the zone and moves below the reaction candle’s low.
  • Acceptance candidate: a 4-hour close above 64,500.
  • Stronger acceptance: the next candle trades mostly above 64,500 or a retest holds above it.
  • No decision: candles overlap inside 64,100–64,500 without clean follow-through.

Replay reveals: price wicks to 64,720 but closes at 64,260. That is rejection evidence, but not the stronger condition. The next candle closes at 64,610 and the following holds above 64,500. Update the interpretation: the initial rejection attempt failed; acceptance now has more support. A paper short entered merely because of the wick would confuse an early clue with a complete setup.

Key lesson: evidence is sequential. Good analysis changes when observations change.

22–27 · Practical: the two-zone constraint

Open BTC/USD in replay mode. Do not place a real order. Hide the future if the platform allows it.

  1. Minute 1: Choose one decision timeframe—4-hour by default—and note exchange, pair, and timestamp.
  2. Minutes 2–3: Mark at most two zones relevant to current price.
  3. Minute 4: Record each source, exact bounds, width percentage, and relevance.
  4. Minute 5: Write observable rejection, acceptance, no-decision, and deletion rules.
  5. Minutes 6–7: Advance 5–10 candles. Classify each interaction; screenshot before and after. Do not move the zone.

LLM feedback artefact: send before/after screenshots and the four fields above. Ask: “Separate observations from interpretations. Did I move a boundary after seeing the outcome? Which classification rule is ambiguous? Do not propose a trade.”

27–30 · Test your reading

1. Price briefly trades above resistance, then closes back inside. Is a breakout confirmed?

2. Three candles close beyond the upper boundary and price trades there for hours. Which label fits: rejection, acceptance, or guaranteed continuation?

3. After seeing the outcome, you widen a zone so the reversal begins inside it. What error is this?

4. Former resistance is crossed and later approached from above. What makes “role reversal” more than a label?

Mastery check

Move on when you can mark no more than two zones without peeking, state exact bounds and deletion rules, distinguish a wick from acceptance, use “unclear” without discomfort, and leave the zone unchanged while replay reveals the result.
