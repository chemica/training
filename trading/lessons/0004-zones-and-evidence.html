Lesson 0004 · 30 minutes · One decision

Define a zone and test acceptance.

By the end: Draw one defensible zone and distinguish rejection, acceptance and unresolved interaction.

Boundary: all charts and tickets are historical, replayed or paper-only. Judge the decision made with information available then—not the eventual P&L.

0–5 min · The invariant

A zone is an area justified before the test. Wicks show trade through it; acceptance requires sustained behaviour beyond it. Exact lines imply precision the market rarely provides.

Zone
Price area linked to prior structural response.
Rejection
Price probes an area and moves away.
Acceptance
Price sustains trade and closes beyond an area.
Role reversal
A prior boundary is tested from the opposite side.

Open the shared glossary if a supporting term is unfamiliar.

5–18 min · One structure, five outcomes

Read each chart left to right. The paths are schematics, not predictions; compare the decisive evidence rather than memorising their silhouettes.

CanonicalExample 1

Repeated 1h closes fail above a zone; price rotates away.

Decision: Evidence supports rejection.

Visual variationExample 2

Price overshoots the zone with a long wick, closes inside, then fails to reclaim.

Decision: Messier candles, same rejection behaviour.

Opposite resolutionExample 3

Price closes above, consolidates there, then holds the retest.

Decision: Acceptance, not rejection; retire the fade thesis.

Near missExample 4

A zone is redrawn wider after price has already reversed.

Decision: Hindsight fitting; the zone did not make an ex-ante decision.

AmbiguousExample 5

One close above is followed by overlapping candles straddling the boundary.

Decision: Unresolved; time outside and follow-through are insufficient.

18–24 min · Unseen decision

Price wicks through resistance, closes just above it, then two candles overlap on both sides. What is established?

24–30 min · Deliberate practice

On a 4h chart, mark one zone before inspecting the lower timeframe. Write width, source, rejection evidence, acceptance evidence and deletion rule. Classify five subsequent interactions without moving it.

Save one pre-outcome screenshot and your classification. Ask your LLM teacher: “Which observation decided the classification? What would falsify it? Ignore the eventual profit.”
