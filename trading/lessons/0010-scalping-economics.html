Lesson 0010 · 30 minutes · One decision

Calculate net scalp expectancy.

By the end: Calculate whether a scalp’s observed payoff distribution remains positive after realistic friction.

Boundary: all charts and tickets are historical, replayed or paper-only. Judge the decision made with information available then—not the eventual P&L.

0–5 min · The invariant

Win rate is not an edge. Expectancy combines win probability, average win, loss probability and average loss after all costs; small targets make estimation error unusually important.

Basis point
0.01%; 100 bp equals 1%.
Hit rate
Proportion of trades that win.
Payoff ratio
Average win divided by average loss.
Expectancy
Average amount expected per trade over a sample.

Open the shared glossary if a supporting term is unfamiliar.

5–18 min · One structure, five outcomes

Read each chart left to right. The paths are schematics, not predictions; compare the decisive evidence rather than memorising their silhouettes.

CanonicalExample 1

60% wins averaging 18 bp; 40% losses averaging 16 bp; friction already deducted.

Decision: Net expectancy = 4.4 bp per trade.

Visual variationExample 2

Only 42% win, but average net win is 35 bp and average net loss 15 bp.

Decision: Net expectancy remains positive: 6 bp.

Opposite resolutionExample 3

75% win 8 bp; 25% lose 30 bp.

Decision: Expectancy is −1.5 bp despite impressive hit rate.

Near missExample 4

Backtest reports gross expectancy of 5 bp but omits 9 bp friction.

Decision: Net expectancy is negative before additional error.

AmbiguousExample 5

Twenty trades show +3 bp average, driven by one outlier and guessed slippage.

Decision: Insufficient evidence; stress assumptions and collect more data.

18–24 min · Unseen decision

A scalp wins 70%: average net win 6 bp, average net loss 18 bp. Expectancy?

24–30 min · Deliberate practice

Use 20 paper outcomes. Calculate gross and net expectancy, then double slippage and reduce hit rate by five percentage points. Do not promote the playbook unless the stressed result remains plausible.

Save one pre-outcome screenshot and your classification. Ask your LLM teacher: “Which observation decided the classification? What would falsify it? Ignore the eventual profit.”
