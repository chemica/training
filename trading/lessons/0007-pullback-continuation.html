Lesson 0007 · 30 minutes · Playbook

Buy evidence of continuation, not a falling price.

Today’s win: turn “the dip looks good” into a falsifiable plan—context, location, trigger, invalidation, and skip conditions—then test it without risking money.

Paper only. This chapter teaches observation and decision structure, not a profitable strategy or personal financial advice. Use chart replay or a simulator: no live orders, no leverage, no adding to losers. Crypto can move sharply, liquidity can disappear, and consumer protection may be limited.

0–4 · Start with uncertainty

At the first counter-trend candle, a pullback and a reversal look identical. Pullback is not a visible object; it is a hypothesis that the prior directional auction will resume. The setup becomes testable only when price supplies evidence and a nearby point at which the hypothesis is wrong.

Retrieval: What separates a pullback from a reversal at the moment it begins?

4–10 · Six terms, one causal story

Impulse

A directional leg that travels efficiently: wider bodies, less overlap, and decisive movement through a prior swing or zone. It establishes the direction you may later trade.

Pullback

A counter-directional retracement within that working trend. Overlap, smaller range, slowing pace, and respect for a meaningful area may support the hypothesis—but none guarantees it.

Continuation

Renewed movement in the impulse direction. Define it with an observable structural event, such as reclaiming a micro swing and holding it—not merely one green candle.

Protected swing

The swing low in an uptrend, or swing high in a downtrend, whose breach would weaken the trend thesis. “Protected” describes your model, not a level the market must defend.

Trigger

An event required before entry: for example, failed selling followed by a close above the last lower-timeframe lower high. It prevents “cheap” becoming the only reason to act.

Invalidation

A prewritten event that makes the thesis false, such as acceptance below the protected swing. It is structural; paper position size is derived from its distance, never vice versa.

10–14 · Read the sequence, not the candle

Scan from large to small. On 4-hour/1-hour, ask whether price trends and where the protected swing lies. On 15-minute/5-minute, watch the correction arrive at a prior breakout, defended swing, or other preselected zone. Only then use a lower-timeframe trigger. Do not let a one-minute pattern overrule a broken higher-timeframe thesis.

14–18 · The playbook

  1. 1 · Context: Directional structure exists: higher highs/higher lows for a long, or the inverse for a short. Mark the protected swing.
  2. 2 · Location: Price returns to a level that mattered before the impulse. A Fibonacci number or moving average alone is not a reason; note what behaviour gives the area relevance.
  3. 3 · Pullback quality: Compare pace, overlap, depth, and volume with the impulse. An accelerating, expansive counter-move is warning evidence—not a bargain.
  4. 4 · Trigger: Write the event before it occurs: “5-minute close above X, then no immediate close back below.” No trigger means no trade.
  5. 5 · Invalidation: Name the structural event that refutes continuation. A stop placed only at a convenient percentage confuses affordable loss with market logic.
  6. 6 · First obstacle: Mark prior high/low, opposing zone, or congestion. Skip if plausible reward to it does not justify risk after spread, fees, and slippage.
paper risk = |hypothetical entry − invalidation|
room to obstacle = |first obstacle − hypothetical entry|
R multiple = room to obstacle ÷ paper risk

18–21 · Worked example: BTC, invented prices

This is not current market data. BTC moves from £50,000 to £52,400, closing above a prior £51,800 ceiling. It retraces in overlapping 5-minute candles into £51,750–£51,900. The last protected 1-hour swing is £51,300; the pullback’s micro lower high is £52,020.

Plan

Trigger: 5-minute close above £52,020, followed by holding above it. Hypothetical entry: £52,050. Invalidation: acceptance below £51,300. Skip if price jumps straight to the prior £52,400 high.

Arithmetic

Paper risk is £750 per BTC. Room to the first obstacle at £52,400 is £350, or 0.47R before costs. That is poor asymmetry, even if the chart looks attractive.

Decision

Pass. A valid-looking trigger does not compel a trade. Location, invalidation distance, and nearby resistance make this candidate unattractive. The lesson is selection, not prediction.

21–24 · Failure modes

  • Catching the knife: entering because price has fallen far, before selling demonstrably fails.
  • Timeframe laundering: calling a 1-hour trend intact after its protected swing breaks because a 1-minute bounce appears.
  • Trigger equals invalidation: entering on a micro reclaim, then exiting on tiny noise at that level. The trigger starts the thesis; invalidation disproves it.
  • Deep-pullback denial: treating stronger counter-trend momentum as a better discount instead of possible regime change.
  • Hindsight drift: moving the zone or rewriting the trigger after seeing the next candle.
  • Ignoring execution: paper profits disappear when spread, fees, slippage, funding, and latency are included.

24–28 · Timed replay drill

Use a liquid BTC spot chart in replay mode; hide all candles to the right. Set a four-minute timer per candidate and complete five candidates over several sessions if needed.

  1. 0:00–0:45: Mark higher-timeframe direction and protected swing.
  2. 0:45–1:30: Mark location and explain why it mattered before today.
  3. 1:30–2:15: Write trigger, invalidation, first obstacle, and one skip condition.
  4. 2:15–3:00: Advance one candle at a time. Record “triggered,” “invalidated,” or “no trade”; never revise the plan.
  5. 3:00–4:00: Screenshot before/after. Score process out of 5: context, location, trigger, invalidation, costs/obstacle.

LLM feedback prompt: “Audit this pre-outcome card. Separate observations from assumptions. Are trigger and invalidation distinct structural events? Identify hindsight, vague terms, and missing costs. Do not predict the market or suggest a live trade.”

28–30 · Test your model

1. Price reaches your zone but never triggers, then rallies. Missed trade?

2. Which is a usable bullish trigger?

A · “Momentum looks better.”

Too vague. It cannot be replayed consistently or audited.

B · “A 5-minute close above the identified micro lower high, followed by a hold.”

Best answer. It names timeframe, level, close, and confirmation. Exact rules can still fail, but can be tested.

C · “Price touched the 50% retracement.”

That describes location, not evidence the counter-move has ended.

3. A long trigger fires, but the first obstacle offers 0.6R before fees. What is disciplined?

Primary reading and evidence

Read CME Group on support, resistance, prior highs/lows, and role reversal. Treat them as areas where behaviour may change, not magic lines. The CFTC virtual-currency advisory explains volatility, platform, manipulation, cyber, and leverage risks; the UK FCA warning explains limited protections and the possibility of total loss. The CFTC also cautions that AI cannot predict sudden market changes—use an LLM to critique written process, never as proof a trade will work.

Graduation rule: remain paper-only until you have a written playbook and a sufficiently large, forward-tested sample including fees and slippage. This chapter intentionally sets no live-money threshold; any later transition needs a separate risk plan based on your finances and jurisdiction.
