Lesson 0005 · 30 minutes · One decision

Estimate executable friction.

By the end: Estimate the round-trip friction of a hypothetical order from spread, fees and plausible slippage.

Boundary: all charts and tickets are historical, replayed or paper-only. Judge the decision made with information available then—not the eventual P&L.

0–5 min · The invariant

Volume is completed activity; liquidity is the ability to execute size near current price. A chart move becomes a tradable edge only after entry and exit friction.

Spread
Best ask minus best bid.
Depth
Displayed quantity available at successive prices.
Slippage
Difference between expected and realised execution.
Round-trip friction
Entry plus exit fees, spread and slippage.

Open the shared glossary if a supporting term is unfamiliar.

5–18 min · One structure, five outcomes

Read each chart left to right. The paths are schematics, not predictions; compare the decisive evidence rather than memorising their silhouettes.

CanonicalExample 1

£1,000 market buy crosses 5 bp spread, pays 10 bp taker fee each side and slips 2 bp each side.

Decision: Estimated round-trip friction: 29 bp if the full spread is crossed once overall.

Visual variationExample 2

High volume prints, but depth near the quote disappears during a fast move.

Decision: High volume did not guarantee cheap immediate execution.

Opposite resolutionExample 3

Quiet trading shows a narrow spread and stable depth for small size.

Decision: Low recent volume can coexist with adequate liquidity for this order size.

Near missExample 4

Only the maker fee is deducted from a market-order backtest.

Decision: Wrong fee class and missing spread/slippage overstate edge.

AmbiguousExample 5

Displayed depth looks large on one venue before a catalyst.

Decision: Cancellation, fragmentation and hidden liquidity leave realised cost uncertain.

18–24 min · Unseen decision

A strategy targets 20 bp gross. Estimated round-trip friction is 24 bp. What follows?

24–30 min · Deliberate practice

At three UK times, record best bid, best ask and displayed depth for the same BTC spot pair. Add the venue’s published fees and a stated slippage assumption to estimate £1,000 round-trip friction.

Save one pre-outcome screenshot and your classification. Ask your LLM teacher: “Which observation decided the classification? What would falsify it? Ignore the eventual profit.”
