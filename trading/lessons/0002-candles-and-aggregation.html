Lesson 0002 · 30 minutes · Price action

A candle is a compression, not a verdict.

Today’s win: reconstruct what OHLC does—and does not—say, then give each timeframe a distinct analytical job.

Practice boundary: use historical replay or paper trading only. Candles do not show every trade, spread, liquidation, or intrabar path, and this chapter provides no personalised signal.

0–3 min · Retrieve before reading

Without looking below, sketch two different price paths with the same open, high, low, and close. Then answer: could a stop at the midpoint survive one path but not the other?

Reveal after sketching

Yes. OHLC records four landmarks, not their sequence. A bar that opened at 100, traded at 105 and 95, then closed at 103 could visit 95 before 105 or 105 before 95. An intrabar entry or stop can therefore have different outcomes even though the finished candle is identical.

3–8 min · The candle’s exact vocabulary

Open (O)
The first recorded trade price in the interval. It is not necessarily the best available bid or ask.
High (H)
The highest recorded trade price during the interval—not proof that much volume traded there.
Low (L)
The lowest recorded trade price during the interval. A single small trade may establish it.
Close (C)
The last recorded trade price in the interval. Crypto trades continuously, so this boundary is imposed by the chart.
Real body
The span between open and close. Its direction says which endpoint is higher, not who “controls” the market.
Wick / shadow
The span from the body to the high or low. It proves the market traded there; “rejection” requires context and follow-through.
Range
H − L. It describes price excursion, not the path or realised volatility by itself.
Aggregation
A rule that groups lower-level trades or bars into a larger interval and retains only O, H, L, and C (often plus volume).
body = |C − O|    upper wick = H − max(O,C)
lower wick = min(O,C) − L    range = H − L

A bullish candle means only C > O; a bearish candle means C < O. Colour is a display convention. Neither statement identifies intent, predicts the next bar, or reveals whether a market order would have filled at the plotted price.

8–14 min · Diagram: one candle, incompatible journeys

What disappeared? Event order, the number and size of trades, time spent at each price, bid–ask spread, order-book depth, gaps in liquidity, and your queue position. Coinbase exposes candles, individual market trades, tickers, and level-two updates as different feeds because each is a different view of the market.[1]

14–18 min · How aggregation works

For a larger bar assembled from consecutive smaller bars, the larger open is the first child’s open; the high is the maximum child high; the low is the minimum child low; and the close is the last child close.

The hourly bar looks bullish with long shadows. The children reveal two-sided rotation and a late recovery. Zooming out improves structural legibility; zooming in improves path detail but multiplies noise and apparent “setups.” CME’s timeframe guide uses multiple scales because they reveal different information, not because one is universally correct.[2]

Boundary trap: candle shapes depend on where intervals begin. A 1-hour bar aligned at 10:00 can look unlike one formed from 10:30–11:30. Exchange timezone, chart timezone, data source, spot-versus-derivative venue, and missing trades can all change the picture.

18–21 min · Assign timeframes jobs

Context · 4h

Classify broad structure and location: trend, range, transition; near a prior swing zone or in the middle?

Setup · 1h

Describe the behaviour worth monitoring: pullback, breakout attempt, failed break, or rotation.

Trigger · 15m

Define an observable condition and invalidation. This scale times a hypothetical entry; it cannot rewrite context.

This is a hierarchy of questions, not a hunt for three charts that agree. A dramatic 15-minute engulfing bar in the centre of a 4-hour range may contain execution detail yet add no directional edge. Support and resistance are better treated as zones than exact prices, preventing one wick from being mistaken for a decisive break.[3]

21–26 min · Worked replay drill

Open a liquid BTC spot chart in replay. Choose a historical date before revealing later bars. Hide indicators.

  1. 4h: mark at most two obvious zones and classify context in one sentence.
  2. 1h: select one complete hourly candle. Record O, H, L, C and calculate body and range.
  3. 15m: inspect its four child bars. Number the swing sequence—for example, open → low → high → close.
  4. Compare: write what the hourly candle hid and what the 15-minute view made noisier.
  5. Predict: before advancing replay, state one observation supporting your interpretation and one invalidating it. Do not place a live order.

26–30 min · Retrieval test

Answer aloud before revealing feedback.

1. A long upper wick proves sellers deliberately defended that exact price. True or false?

2. Child highs are 102, 105, 104, 103; lows are 99, 100, 98, 95. What are the hourly high and low?

3. A bullish 15-minute engulfing candle appears mid-range on 4h. Is that alone a complete long setup?

4. Name three details a completed candle cannot recover.

Feedback artefact

Save two screenshots of the same interval—4h and 15m—plus five lines: context, large-bar reading, hidden path, trigger condition, invalidation. Ask your LLM teacher:

Critique whether I separated observation from inference, reconstructed aggregation correctly, and gave each timeframe one job. Do not judge by subsequent profit and do not provide a trade signal.
