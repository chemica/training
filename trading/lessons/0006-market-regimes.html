Lesson 0006 · 30 minutes · paper only

Choose the game before the move.

Today’s win: describe the market’s current behaviour, select a compatible playbook—or deliberately take no trade—without pretending to predict the next candle.

Boundary: this chapter is practice, not a recommendation to trade. Use chart replay or a paper account. Crypto trades continuously, can move sharply, and may lack protections available in regulated securities markets; read the CFTC virtual-currency advisory before risking money.

0–4 · Retrieve before reading

Without looking ahead, write one sentence for each:

  1. What visible evidence separates a trend from a range?
  2. Why might the same pullback setup work on Tuesday and fail on Wednesday?
  3. What would make “no trade” the best-scoring decision?

Leave your answers visible. At the end, revise them in a different colour.

4–10 · Two axes, not one label

A market regime is a useful description of the behaviour dominating the chosen instrument and timeframe now. It is not a permanent property of BTC, nor a forecast. Classify on two independent axes:

  • Structure: directional when price repeatedly makes progress one way; rotational when price repeatedly traverses and rejects a bounded area.
  • Volatility: compressed when recent ranges and swings are small relative to their own recent history; expanded when they are large. Volatility measures movement, not direction.
Directional + compressedOrderly drift. Pullbacks may be shallow; fees can consume small targets.
Directional + expandedFast trend. More movement, wider invalidation, greater slippage risk.
Rotational + compressedTight balance. Few clean moves; often wait for expansion.
Rotational + expandedWide, violent range. Boundary fades may work, but false breaks are common.

Transition is the practical third state: evidence is changing or contradictory. A range may be attempting a breakout; a trend may be losing progress. During transition, reduce confidence instead of forcing the old label.

10–15 · Observe, then classify

Use a fixed evidence window—say, the last 30 completed candles—and describe only what is visible. Do not let a preferred setup choose the regime for you.

  1. Progress: are successive swings and closes advancing, or repeatedly retracing?
  2. Overlap: do bars occupy fresh territory, or overlap the same prices?
  3. Acceptance: after leaving a level, do closes hold outside or return inside?
  4. Volatility: compare median true range now with an earlier equal-length window. “High” must be relative.
  5. Conflict: if observations disagree, label transition/unclear and reduce size to zero in this exercise.

True range = max(high − low, |high − prior close|, |low − prior close|)

Average True Range (ATR) summarizes true ranges but does not say whether price will rise or fall. See Fidelity’s ATR guide. A higher timeframe supplies context; the execution timeframe supplies the setup. Record any conflict explicitly.

18–21 · Worked example

Scene: BTC/USD, 15-minute chart. Across 30 completed bars, price makes three higher swing lows and two higher highs. Breaks above prior swing highs hold for several closes. Candle overlap is moderate. Median true range over the last 10 bars is 1.6× the preceding 20-bar median. The 4-hour chart is also advancing.

Reasoning: repeated one-way progress plus acceptance outside prior highs outweigh moderate overlap: directional. Relative range increased: expanded volatility. Timeframes agree: no conflict flag.

Decision: permit only a paper trend-continuation playbook, demanding a defined pullback and invalidation. Do not chase an extended candle. If price closes back into the broken area and erases the latest higher low, reclassify as transition. Classification controls eligibility; it does not create an entry.

21–27 · Timed chart-replay lab

  1. 1 minute: open BTC/USD replay on 15m; hide the right edge; choose 30 completed bars.
  2. 2 minutes: mark swings, overlap, held/failed breaks, and relative range. Use no indicators except volume and optional ATR.
  3. 1 minute: run the classifier and write: “I see ___ / therefore ___ / invalid if ___.”
  4. 2 minutes: reveal five bars. Score the evidence and decision—not P&L or direction.

27–30 · Test and feedback

1. Price repeatedly breaks a range edge, then closes back inside. Best label?

2. ATR rises sharply. What has it established by itself?

3. Your setup is valid, but timeframe evidence conflicts. Best paper decision?

Suggested retrieval answers

A trend shows sustained progress and acceptance; a range shows overlap, two-way travel, and failed acceptance beyond boundaries. A pullback setup can fail after regime change because its assumptions no longer match behaviour. No-trade is correct when classification is unclear, playbook and regime conflict, costs erase the edge, or invalidation cannot be defined.
