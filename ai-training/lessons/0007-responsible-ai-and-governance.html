Lesson 0007 · AIF-C01 domains 4 and 5

Control the harm.

Learn responsible-AI properties, data governance, privacy, security, human oversight, and the difference between a principle and an implemented control.

45–60 minutes · AWS Certified AI Practitioner

1 · Orient

Prerequisite: training, inference, foundation model, prompt, and RAG.

Outcome: You can identify a plausible AI risk, connect it to an affected person, select a preventive or detective control, and define evidence that the control works.

Theme · Why useful AI also needs boundaries

An AI feature changes more than application behaviour: it changes who may be affected by uncertain outputs and who must answer when those outputs cause harm. A polished response can conceal bias, invented facts, private data, or a failure that disproportionately affects one group.

For a support copilot, the model may recommend priority and draft a reply, but a person can retain authority over consequential decisions. Data classification determines what may enter the system. Governance assigns owners and approval rules. Evaluation supplies evidence, while monitoring reveals whether real use differs from testing.

The objective is not to promise a risk-free model. It is to identify plausible harms, apply proportionate controls, and document residual limitations honestly.

2 · Learn responsible-AI properties

Fairness
Concern with unjust differences in outcomes across people or groups.
Bias
A systematic tendency in data, design, or model behavior that can produce distorted outcomes.
Explainability
The degree to which reasons for a model output can be understood.
Transparency
Clear communication about system purpose, data, limitations, and AI involvement.
Robustness
The ability to behave acceptably under variation, noise, misuse, or attack.
Toxicity
Harmful, abusive, or inappropriate generated content.

These properties can conflict. A more detailed explanation can expose sensitive information. A stronger safety filter can reject legitimate requests. Responsible design makes trade-offs explicit and testable.

3 · Learn governance and data terms

Governance is the system of decisions, ownership, policies, controls, and evidence used to direct and oversee AI. Compliance is conformity with applicable laws, regulations, contracts, and policies. They are related but not identical.

Data classification assigns categories based on sensitivity and handling requirements. Privacy concerns appropriate processing of personal data. Encryption protects data confidentiality with cryptographic keys, but it does not decide whether the use itself is lawful or appropriate.

Human oversight gives an accountable person a meaningful ability to review, intervene, or override. A decorative approval button is not meaningful oversight if the reviewer lacks information or time.

4 · Learn controls and evidence

A preventive control tries to stop an unwanted event. Least-privilege IAM and input limits are examples. A detective control identifies an event or condition. Audit logs and quality alarms are examples. A corrective control restores an acceptable state, such as disabling a model route or rolling back a prompt version.

A risk statement should name the condition, event, impact, and affected party. A control should have an owner and test evidence.

Worked example

An AI system drafts customer cancellation responses. Risk: the model may invent contract terms, causing financial harm and regulatory complaints.

Prevent: retrieve approved contract clauses, require cited source identifiers, restrict tools, and prohibit autonomous sending. Detect: evaluate factual consistency and log source coverage. Correct: route low-confidence drafts to manual handling and roll back a failing prompt version. Human oversight: an authorized agent reviews the draft and source before sending.

6 · Apply it

Choose a fictional AI document summarizer. Write two risk statements. For each, identify the affected party, one preventive control, one detective control, one corrective response, and the evidence an auditor could inspect.

7 · Review

The tutor will challenge vague claims such as “use responsible AI.” Every principle must become a concrete control with evidence and ownership.
