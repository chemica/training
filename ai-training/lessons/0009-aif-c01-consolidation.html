1 · Orient

Prerequisite: Lessons 0003–0008. This lesson introduces no new assessed terminology.

Outcome: You can move from a business outcome to an appropriate AI approach, AWS controls, and measurable evidence without selecting technology first.

Theme · Why the domains must work together

Real AI decisions do not arrive labelled with an exam domain. A customer asks for faster ticket handling; you must decide whether AI is justified, which capability fits, what data may be used, how output quality will be measured, and which controls make deployment acceptable.

This lesson joins the earlier ideas into one decision sequence. Business value defines success. AI and generative-AI concepts identify feasible approaches. Evaluation distinguishes a promising demo from repeatable performance. Responsible-AI and security controls determine whether the system deserves to be used.

The foundation build is therefore a small professional proposal, not merely a model call. Its evidence should allow another person to challenge the recommendation.

2 · Learn the decision sequence

  1. Outcome: Define the user, decision or task, and measurable improvement.
  2. AI fit: Decide whether rules, traditional ML, or generative AI is appropriate.
  3. Data: Identify sources, labels, sensitivity, quality, ownership, and permitted use.
  4. Technique: Choose prompting, RAG, fine-tuning, or a non-generative approach.
  5. Controls: Define access, safety, privacy, human oversight, audit, and recovery.
  6. Evaluation: Compare against a baseline using representative cases.
  7. Operation: Measure quality, latency, cost, and failures after deployment.

The exam can ask for the most appropriate service or approach. Read every constraint. The technically possible answer is not always the answer that best satisfies risk, cost, or operational requirements.

3 · See the domain connections

AI/ML fundamentals

Name the learning problem, inputs, outputs, labels, inference behavior, and metric.

Generative AI

Account for tokens, context, model capability, inference parameters, and adaptation method.

FM applications

Design prompts, retrieval, model selection, structured output, and evaluation.

Responsible AI

Evaluate fairness, robustness, transparency, explainability, and harmful output.

Security and governance

Control identities, data, encryption, audit evidence, ownership, and human decisions.

Worked example

A firm wants to reduce the time staff spend finding answers in approved installation manuals.

Outcome: correct cited answer in under 30 seconds. Fit: generative explanation is helpful, but facts must come from current manuals. Technique: RAG with approved documents, not fine-tuning for document facts. Controls: staff authentication, document-level authorization, prompt-injection tests, citations, human escalation, audit events, and data retention. Evaluation: representative answerable and unanswerable questions; measure retrieval recall, groundedness, citation correctness, latency, and cost. Operation: monitor failures and document freshness.
